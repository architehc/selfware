# Applications and evidence reading notes

Source: *The Last AI Built by Humans: Toward Genuine Recursive Self-Improvement*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11873. Read printed pp. 36–50 and appendix PDF pp. 67–75. Tables 9 and 10 and Figure 16 also checked visually. Statements below distinguish the paper's reports from our assessment; external cited systems have not been independently replicated or audited.

## Central takeaway

The application chapters repeatedly distinguish a better current artifact from a persistent improvement to the agent, and distinguish both from an improved future improvement process. Cheap executable feedback makes software an attractive setting, but the paper explicitly says full software L5 has not been demonstrated (p. 42). The industry chapter is a collection of preliminary reports with heterogeneous evidence, not a demonstration of an integrated general RSI system.

## Applications, pp. 36–44

- **Science (36–38):** What evolves can be the hypothesis generator, experimental agent, or improver. The key constraints are expensive experiments, ambiguous failure attribution, and validity limited by assumptions and domain. Stronger evidence is successful reuse on unseen tasks, not mere library growth. DrugSAGE reportedly transfers experience from 16 tasks to 17 held-out tasks (37). The authors assess L2 as the strongest current frontier; L4 and L5 remain largely unexplored (38).
- **Embodied intelligence (38–40):** Curricula/environments, skills/harnesses, policies, world models, and evaluators can co-evolve. Current actions determine which future experiences are available; physical experiments also resist reset and rollback. EmbodiSkill distinguishes bad skills from an agent failing to follow a valid skill (39), a useful attribution principle. POET and environment-generation systems show stronger adaptation in simulation. ENPIRE permits edits to robot policies, training procedures, and infrastructure, but fixed external interfaces, evaluators, and safety bounds limit its L5 interpretation (40).
- **Software (40–42):** Both the product and the agent are executable code. Authors propose development challenge → persistent agent modification → repository trial → regression-aware selection → versioned inheritance. Software tasks must be valuable as well as verifiable, and immediate benchmark gains must be distinguished from ability to produce better descendants.
  - SICA edits its own agent code, with benchmark/utility/sandbox fixed. Self-Harness uses traces and regression tests to retain small harness edits. Agentic Harness Engineering uses independently editable/revertible components and predicted-effect checks. Ouroboros moves reviewed modifications into subsequent deployed runtime (41).
  - SWE-Exp retains successful and failed trajectories; CODESKILL manages procedural knowledge. Their extraction/update procedures remain fixed. EvoMAC evolves role prompts and communication, but updates are task-local (41).
  - DGM keeps multiple lineages so weaker present variants can become stepping stones. HGM estimates descendant metaproductivity, but its meta-selection rule does not itself adapt. DarwinX requires increased task coverage without regressions. HELIX couples harness evolution with later model training (41–42).
  - Authors' verdict: L2 established; L3 emerging; L4 largely absent; SICA/DGM have bounded L5 characteristics, but full L5 is unproven. User intent, evaluation constraints, auditability, and rollback stay externally protected (42).
- **Healthcare (42–44):** Memory, reasoning policies, and tools/workflows evolve, but most evidence uses simulated or retrospective cases and supplied labels. Prospective real-patient longitudinal evidence is missing. Technical task success does not establish transfer across populations. L2 is the strongest current frontier; real-world end-to-end L4 and clinical-improver L5 are largely unexplored.

## Industry evidence, pp. 44–50

| Case | Paper's reported evidence | Assessment of what it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| Theseus (44–45, Table 9) | Eight model/harness configurations, 30 workspace tasks and 1,280 rubrics: clean versus noisy workspace gains of 21.7–51.6 percentage points. Separate five-configuration study: 30 tasks, 547 rubrics; adding Collection Map/Event Log gains 18.65–39.67 points. | Strong environment/context effect in these small pilots; not yet evidence of a sustained environment–data–model co-evolution cycle. Authors call these first steps. Some tasks regress through excessive scope. No uncertainty intervals or repeated-run variance shown here. |
| Lark (45–46) | Knowledge-graph pipeline versus RAG: human-rated usability 52→65%; automatic usability 47→56%; evaluator/human agreement about 84%. | Evidence for data/retrieval infrastructure, evaluator calibration, and human-gated improvement. These figures do not isolate autonomous recursive meta-improvement. |
| Humanlaya (46–47) | Four feedback-driven updates, V0 versus V4, same model/tools/budget, 600 held-out task packages: defects after repair 9.0→3.7%; human handling 48→27 minutes. | Clearest case here of several persistent scaffold updates plus a controlled held-out comparison. Still internally reported and human-approved, without uncertainty estimates or ablations identifying which update caused what share of gain. |
| ModelBest (47–48) | ForgeTrain reportedly matched Megatron-LM v0.15 on H100 within ~8 hours, surpassed within 1.5–2.5 days. MFU 40.1→44.1% (0.5B), 47.0→50.9% (8B). ForgeStencil reported median 1.41× end-to-end speedup. | Useful engineering output/performance claims; does not by itself isolate benefit of cross-project inheritance or improved improver. Comparison to 3–5 engineers over 6–12 months is an estimate, not measured matched human-control evidence. “Zero-human” project delivery coexists with a human-and-AI-maintained knowledge base. |
| Tencent Hyra (48–49, Table 10) | Experience Bank keeps code/artifacts/logs/evaluations; parallel agents use diverse contexts. Reported BPB 0.9109→0.9015, training time 77.5→76.4 s, mean SOL 0.754→0.771. | Demonstrates reported solution-search results. No comparison here with the same search minus inherited memory, or fixed versus adaptive evaluator, isolating the recursive mechanism. Resource matching and statistical uncertainty are not described in this section. Table marks 0.771 with a dagger whose explanation is not visible on p49. |
| Agent-Native Research Lab (49–50) | Executable research artifacts preserve assumptions, code, failed paths, and raw evidence. Reported QA 72.4→93.7%, RE-Bench reproduction 57.4→64.4%. Silicon design: 2.7% faster, 21.5% less area, passing 97 directed and 350 random differential programs. | Supports the potential value of richer inheritance and deterministic checks. Sample size/protocol details for QA/reproduction are not supplied here. Correct logs/formal checks protect specific claims, not all scientific interpretation or future generalization. |

Humanlaya's illustrative bug: a clear scanned PDF was labeled unusable because extraction failed. The outer-loop fix teaches future checkers to distinguish tool failure from document quality (47). This is a particularly concrete example of persistent attribution improvement rather than repeated artifact repair.

## Appendix, PDF pp. 67–75

- Figure 16 (67): 491 surveyed papers, classified L1 215 (43.8%), L2 155 (31.6%), L3 64 (13.0%), L4 28 (5.7%), L5 29 (5.9%). L1+L2 account for 75.4% by rounded percentages. Improvement-target counts can have fractional weights when a paper touches multiple targets.
- These are counts of assigned autonomy categories, **not** empirical probabilities, capability measurements, or 29 demonstrations of full genuine L5. Distinguish this taxonomy count from the main text's narrower criteria for full RSI.
- Table 11 (67–69) enumerates editable targets: prompts/context; memory; harness/workflow; tools/skills; model; trainer; evaluator; data/environment; external artifacts; and whole-system/meta loops. Useful to locate what changes, but does not establish that changing it helps future improvement.
- Table 12 (70–75) catalogs 72 companies/teams and representative products. It explicitly marks adjacent infrastructure, candidate levels, future targets, and uncertain company identities. “L5 target” and “L5 candidate” must not be flattened into demonstrated L5.
- Concrete classification distinction: AlphaEvolve is B0 in this table because the classified activity is task-specific program search; an autonomous coding/research agent need not improve itself. OpenAI Deep Research and Anthropic Multi-agent Research are also B0 as task-local evidence generation (70).
- The appendices add landscape organization, not detailed supplementary methods, statistical analyses, longitudinal trajectories, or raw data for the six industry case studies.

## Three discussion questions

1. What should count as the inherited change: a useful memory, an improved coding harness, or a demonstrably more effective mechanism for making the next improvement?
2. What experiment would distinguish compounding improvement from simply running a fixed search procedure longer—especially for DGM/HGM and the industry examples?
3. If the agent may rewrite its evaluator, which independent acceptance criterion preserves user intent and makes old-versus-new measurements comparable?
